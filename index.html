Data & Digital Services / MARC

From a question
to a published story

A working map of how a data story actually gets made here, from the first question to the post that ships. It is a loop, not a line. At every stage there are three honest moves: keep going, change direction, or set it down.

Inspired by https://pudding.cool/process/pivot-continue-down/

Three checks to data story logic

Continue
The work holds up. Move to the next stage.
Pivot
Something is off. Reframe, re-source, or redesign.
Put it down
Pause or stop. A valid call, not a failure.
1

Stage 01

Frame the question

Do I have a unique question data can answer?

Start with a question, not a dataset. Scope it to a specific angle - "why do we not have enough housing?" can be narrowed to "why do we not have enough single-family housing for [ex: public workers] demographic in [overland park]. If someone has already answered it and it feels incomplete, consider what's missing from their findings [ex: "Findings show workers in this income bracket can't afford the housing needed for a family based on median home values"] Pivot to what else can be considered [ex: Workers can't find afford housing needed for a family within their income range, but this is due to childcare expenses in XYZ neighborhood].

Checks

ContinueThe question is regional, MARC's involvement changes something, and data can plausibly answer it.
PivotIt has been asked before. Reframe it. "Why are renters struggling" becomes "who actually rents in this region, and where is the squeeze."
Put it downIt is not regional and there is no value add (requests from the public, other programs, constituents) to warrant resources towards it.

Tools & resources

  • BIG IDEA worksheet. Forces a single-sentence point of view, names the one person you are talking to, and spells out what is at stake.
  • Systems Thinking worksheet. four lenses are considered (system, connection, leverage, time) to find the leverage point of a research question: the thing that is underexplored or that stakeholders keep asking about.
  • MARC scope check. Is it regional? Does MARC make a difference? Do stakeholders want the involvement? Are there resources?
from previous/current work
The Profile of a Renter narrative started from age-cohort missing-household data and became a four-part arc about who actually rents in the region. The LIHTC preservation question, "how many affordable units could roll off," scoped straight to a number people could act on.

Stage 02

Source & process the data

Do the data exist, and can I get them into usable shape?

Leverage data that already exists before pursuing new information. If a public source fits the question, use it. If not, build the dataset if needed and brings value. Budget time for this.

Checks

ContinueA credible source exists or you can compile/create one.
PivotThe source does not fit the question. Swap sources, or adjust the question to the data you can defend.
Put it downThe data do not exist, are not usable, or it is not ethical to use them.

Where to look, in order

  • MARC data catalog first. The MARC_CENSUS SDE tables (ACS, CHAS, decennial) and the enterprise geodatabases. Start in house.
  • mySidewalk. Seek for quick exploration and pre-built indicators when you need a fast read on whether something is there.
  • Public sources. Census API, IPUMS PUMS microdata, NHPD and LIHTC, HUD CHAS, DOE LEAD. Market data from CoStar, Apartments.com and Dodge. State agencies like KHRC and MHDC.
  • Pull and shape. Python and pandas for API sourcing and cleaning. ArcGIS Pro and Portal for anything spatial.
  • Hosting. The X drive for data. The CHIEFS server for enterprise GIS (MARC_PUB).
from previous/current work
The streetcar corridor analysis merged CoStar, Apartments.com and Dodge into one 194-record geodataset. The BikeRepairStands pipeline moved from a file GDB to the MARC_PUB enterprise GDB on CHIEFS with a truncate-and-append. The underproduction pipeline pulled ACS 1-year estimates and PUMS microdata against a 2000 decennial baseline.

Stage 03

Validate the methodology

Is the method vetted by other program experts, documented and reproducible?

This is the stage that states assumptions with defensible rationale. If the methodology is handed off to someone with no background on it, they should be able to recreate it.

What is a strong methodology

  1. Borrow a published framework instead of inventing one. The underproduction work replicates the UpForGrowth headship-rate model, so the method arrives already peer-reviewed.
  2. Set a defensible baseline. 2000 decennial headship rates by age cohort, 15 to 54 as primary and 55 to 64 as the MARC extension.
  3. Define the universe out loud. Know exactly what ACS counts versus what PUMS counts, and where CHAS universes differ. Disaggrecheck tenure on purpose, not by accident.
  4. Debug for assumptions and calculations. Real ones caught in underproduction methodology: group-quarters inflation from IPUMS, the actualUnitsAvailable formula that cancelled out, PUMA vintage boundary mismatches, AMI bucket thresholds, and the ArcPy script's hardcoded year list and wrong assisted-units field.
  5. Cross-validate. Check ACS against PUMS on the same tenure-by-age cut before you trust either one.
  6. Get it reviewed. Refer to the original methodology Walk them through the assumptions and output.
  7. Log the gaps. Be explicit in what you do not have yet when viewing the numbers.

Checks

ContinueThe method survives review and the cross-checks line up.
PivotAn assumption breaks. Revise the model or redefine the universe, the way correcting the AMI buckets brought the LIHTC roll-off down to a defensible 13,257 units.
Put it downData gaps make the result unreliable. Pause until the gap closes rather than publish a number you cannot stand behind.

Stage 04

Visualize

What is the simplest visual that makes the one insight land in ten seconds?

Exploring for yourself is a different job than explaining to a reader.

Tool, and when to use it

Excel
quick exploratory charts and calc checks. A bar or line graph you screenshot for internal review. Not for publishing.
Python
Exploratory analysis and anything you will repeat across years or geographies. Small multiples, programmatic, reproducible.
Everviz
WYSIWYG, MARC-branded, embeddable. This is the publish-ready chart for the blog and web (mySidewalk Chart does the same job).
ArcGIS Pro
Anything spatial. Choropleths, point maps, corridor overlays, and map-heavy stories through StoryMaps.
Shorthand: exploratory goes to Excel or Python. Explanatory and published goes to Everviz for charts, ArcGIS for maps.

Checks

ContinueThe visual passes the five-second test. A reader gets the point before they read the caption.
PivotChange the chart type. The guide's before and after: a crowded pie becomes a sorted bar with direct labels.
Put it downProse says it better. A visual that adds complexity without insight is one you can cut.
Guide to lean on
Data-Visualization-Guide.pdf for the chart-type picker by data type, the MARC color palette with hex values, type and accessibility rules, the five-second test, exploratory versus explanatory, and the pie-versus-bar before and after.
from previous/current work
The choropleth of high-burden, pre-1980 tracts for the modular data story. Renter surplus by age band in the underproduction analysis as direct-labeled bars in the renter profile.

Stage 05

Narrate & publish

Does the story move from data to implication, and is it ready for the room?

The number is not the story. The story is what the number means for a decision someone in the region has to make.

The blog template

  • Framing the issue. Civic, factual, why the reader should care.
  • Why it matters. Data to implication, tied to regional goals and planning decisions.
  • What the data shows. The findings, factually, with one to three visuals.
  • Learn more and next steps. Tools, dashboards, related stories, a forward-looking hook.
  • Methodology, as needed. The short version of Stage 03.

The ideal cadence

  • Department/program specialists scopes the question in week 1. Gather and build data from weeks 1 to 2. Department/program specialists draft through weeks 3 to 4 and visuals in week 5. Review and publishing weeks 7-9.

Before it ships: the Reflect & Iterate check

  • Did this illuminate the system, or just describe symptoms?
  • Did it support MARC's role: convening, informing, influencing?
  • Who needs to hear this, and are they actually in the audience?

Checks

ContinueIt answers the question and serves MARC's role. Publish it.
PivotThe storyboard falls flat. Same data, new frame.
Put it downIt missed its window or its a story better told by someone else.

Iterative, not linear

The Pudding's point holds here: there are more honest paths to put it down than to publish, and that is fine. Pivoting and pausing are not failure, they are how the published work gets good.

Any stage can send you back a step. A broken assumption in Stage 03 can resend you to Stage 02. A flat storyboard in Stage 05 can resend you to Stage 01. When that happens, write the decision down.
